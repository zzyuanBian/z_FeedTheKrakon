## 1. Players / Teams

**Valid player counts: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11.** ("TEAM COMPOSITIONS OVERVIEW" table, printed on pp.5 and 7.)

| Players: | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sailor:** | 3 **or** 2 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| **Pirates:** | 1 **or** 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 |
| **Cult Leader:** | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| **Cultist:** | – | – | – | – | – | – | 1 |

**5 PLAYER RULES** (verbatim, p.5): "When playing with 5 players, the team composition can vary: There are either 3 Sailors, 1 Pirate and 1 Cult Leader or 2 Sailors, 2 Pirates and 1 Cult Leader in the game. To realize this, prepare bags with 3 Sailor and 2 Pirate faction chips and shuffle the bags carefully. Now remove a random bag without revealing the content. Then add the bag with the Cult Leader chip and shuffle the bags again. Each player receives one bag as usual. During the secret pirate gathering the Pirates will realize how many they actually are. All other players try to find out the actual team constellation on the ship during the game."

Note the asymmetric information engineered by that rule: with 5 players the *Pirates* (via the secret gathering) know whether the game is 3S/1P or 2S/2P; the Sailors do not.

### Starting resources

- **Guns: "Each player receives 3 guns."** (p.4 Quick Journey step 6; p.6 Long Journey step 6.) Identical at every player count. 40 gun components total. No faction starts with a different gun count — the "how many guns each faction starts with" framing does not apply; all players start with 3.
- **Résumés: 0 at start.** No player begins with a résumé. Résumés accumulate one per navigation won by that player as captain (§5). The captain's logbook starts empty.
- **What scales with player count:** (a) the team-composition table above; (b) number of **off-duty signs** (see below); (c) the **mutiny gun threshold** (3/4/5 guns depending on count — §6); (d) the set of roles that receive off-duty signs each round (§4).
- **Note on 5 players:** 3 Sailors + 1 Pirate + 1 Cult Leader = 5 chips, but the rulebook says to prepare bags with **3 Sailor and 2 Pirate** chips (5 + Cult Leader = 6 bags) and remove a random one before dealing. So 6 bags are prepared for a 5-player game, one is discarded unseen.

### Off-duty signs set aside at setup

| Number of players | 5–6 | 7–8 | 9–11 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number of off-duty signs | 1 | 2 | 3 |

(p.4, Quick Journey. p.6 Long Journey gives the same table restricted to 7–8 → 2 and 9–11 → 3.)

### Map selection

- **Quick Journey map:** "Use the quick journey map for 5, 6 and 7 player games. Regardless of player count, you should play your first game on this map, just to get a feeling for the game."
- **Long Journey map:** "Use the long journey map for games with 7 or more players." / "Once you feel familiar with the game, you may switch to the long journey map with all the components for games with 7+ players."
- (Overlap at exactly 7 players is intentional; 7 works on both.)

## 2. The Three Factions and Their Victory Conditions

Verbatim from "Victory Conditions", p.8:

- **Sailors** (blue anchor): "All Sailors win as a team if the ship reaches Bluewater Bay, the blue area in the east."
- **Pirates** (red skull): "All Pirates win as a team if the ship reaches Crimson Cove, the red area in the west."
- **Cult Leader & Cultists** (yellow octopus / green tentacle): "The Cult wins (as a team) if the ship reaches the enormous Kraken in the north (Cult Victory) or when the Cult Leader is sacrificed to the Kraken on one of the Feed the Kraken spaces."

**Secondary / "who wins if" rules:**

- **Team-shared victory and eliminated players:** "Players always win together with their team - even if they went overboard before the end of the game." (p.8) — being eliminated does not forfeit your win.
- **Cult Leader fed to the Kraken:** "If the Cult Leader is selected to be fed to the Kraken, the Cult Leader and all of their Cultists immediately win the game." (p.14, Feed the Kraken, *Important*) — this is the "sacrificed to the Kraken" half of the Cult victory condition.
- **Cult Leader cannot win by going overboard:** "The Cult Leader can **not** win by jumping overboard as the *navigator*, even if this happens on a *Feed the Kraken* space." (p.12)
- **Converted players:** "The new Cultist loses their previous team membership and is no longer a Pirate or Sailor. They can only win together with the Cult Leader by fulfilling one of the cult victory conditions." (p.15)
- **Reveal at end:** "Players reveal their faction at the end of the game by showing each other the faction chips from their sea bags." (p.13)

**Ambiguity flagged:** the rulebook gives **no** tie-break or shared-victory rule between two different factions (there is only ever one destination reached, so no such rule is needed). There is also no scoring system — victory is purely binary by team. Nothing in the book defines a "second place".

## 3. The Ship / Map

- **Board:** "1 Game Board — Double-sided - Quick Journey & Long Journey" (p.2). Two boards, one per journey length.
- **Spaces:** the board is a **hex grid**. The rulebook **never states an exact space count**. It must be transcribed from the board art. **FLAGGED AS A GAP** — this is also the reason for the project's existing "Map topology blocker" memory note (per-space arrow directions can't be read from the rulebook; they must be transcribed from the physical board).
- **Start space:** "Place the ship on the starting area in the south." (p.4, p.6 step 1). The start is off the southern edge of the map.
- **Coloured arrows = movement direction, keyed to card colour.** Each map space carries a coloured arrow (yellow, blue or red). A navigation card's colour determines which arrow is used: "Move the ship in the direction of the colored arrow on the current map space, which corresponds to the color of the navigation card." (p.11, step I.)
  - **Yellow** = **North** (see §5 deck table: "North (yellow)").
  - **Blue** = **East** ("East (blue)").
  - **Red** = **West** ("West (red)").
- **Win regions:**
  - **Bluewater Bay** — "the blue area in the east" → **Sailors**.
  - **Crimson Cove** — "the red area in the west" → **Pirates**.
  - **The enormous Kraken** — "in the north" → **Cult**.
  - All three are shown as white outlined hexes at the board's north / east / west edges; on p.4/p.6 the east hex is labelled **"PIRATE VICTORY"** and the west hex **"SAILOR VICTORY"** — see the contradiction note below.
- **Special spaces (map action tokens/miniatures placed at setup):**
  - **Cabin Search** (magnifying glass icon) — Quick Journey **3×**; Long Journey **4×**.
  - **Feed the Kraken** (octopus icon) — Quick Journey **2×**; Long Journey **3×**.

  - Total map action tokens in the box: **7** ("7 Map Action Tokens — 4 Cabin Search, 2 Flogging, 1 Off with the Tongue", p.2) plus **3 Kraken miniatures ("Feed the Kraken")** in the deluxe edition (p.3); the basic edition has 3 additional Feed the Kraken map action tokens instead.
- **Quick Journey token set (p.4):** "Place the *map action tokens/miniatures* on the corresponding areas on the map: 3x Cabin Search, 2x Feed the Kraken."
- **Long Journey token set (p.6):** "Place the *map action tokens* on the corresponding areas on the map: 4x Cabin Search, 2x Flogging, 1x Off with the Tongue, 3x Feed the Kraken."

- **Supply Line (Long Journey map only):** "The supply line only exists on the *long journey* map. As soon as the ship crosses the supply line, each player can **refill** their gun supply **up to** three guns. To do so players take the required number of guns from the general supply. If a player still has three or more guns at this point, they will **not** receive any additional guns." (p.12)

The **text is authoritative** for the engine; but this must be verified against the physical board. It is genuinely inconsistent in the photographed pages (pp.4, 5, 6, 7).

## 4. Turn / Round Structure

"The game is divided into the following recurring phases:" (p.9)

1. **Appointing the navigation team**
2. **A question of loyalty**
   2.1. **Resolving a mutiny**
3. **The navigation**
   3.1. **Executing the Navigation Card**
4. **Off-duty**
   4.1. **The next game round begins**

The loop repeats from phase 1 until a victory condition is met ("The navigation rounds are repeated until one of the victory conditions is met", p.13).

### Phase 1 — Appointing the Navigation Team (p.9)

"The captain cannot navigate the ship alone. He needs the support of two crew members. After potentially discussing with the crew whom to appoint:"
- "The *captain* chooses a player and hands them the *lieutenant* badge."
- "The *captain* chooses a second player and hands them the *navigator* badge."

**"The following exceptions must be noted:"**
- "The *captain* can **not** appoint himself."
- "*Off-duty* players can **not** be appointed."
- "*Lieutenant* and *navigator* can **not** be the same person."

The captain also holds the captain's logbook, lieutenant badge and navigator badge (p.8).

### Phase 2 — A Question of Loyalty (p.9)

"The crew does not have to accept the captain's choices. If the crew doesn't trust the *captain's* chosen team, they can **mutiny** against them with their guns."
"The captain has to ask the crew about their loyalty: *"If you are not satisfied with my decision, let your guns speak!"*"

**Steps carried out (p.9):**
1. "Each crew member (except the captain) secretly places 0 or more of their guns in one fist. The unused guns remain hidden in their other hand."
2. "All crew members hold their closed fist visibly over the table to show that they have decided."
3. "The captain counts to 3: *"Show me your loyalty! 1, ... 2, ... 3!"*"
4. "All players open their fists and potentially reveal their guns."
5. "The captain counts the total amount of revealed guns and checks *The Crew* card to see if the mutiny is successful."

**Mutiny threshold table (p.9):**

| Players | Required sum of guns for a successful mutiny |
|---|---|
| 5-7 | 3 or more guns |
| 8-9 | 4 or more guns |
| 10-11 | 5 or more guns |

"**You can also find the required number of guns (for a successful mutiny) on *The Crew* card.**"
"**The number of guns each player has is secret information *during* the mutiny. Outside of the mutiny, however, this is a public information and all players must keep their guns visibly on the table at all times.**" (p.9)

### Phase 3 — The Navigation (p.10)

"The captain, lieutenant and navigator must now navigate the ship together as a team. The navigation is carried out as follows:"
1. "The *captain* draws the top **two** navigation cards **face down** from the draw pile."
2. "The *lieutenant* then draws the next **two** cards **face down** from the draw pile."
3. "Both players look at their cards and choose one of them to discard **face down** into the deep sea (the discard pile)."
4. "Then they each place their other card face down and next to each other in the captain's logbook."
5. "Close and shake the *captain's logbook* to shuffle the two cards. The *captain's logbook* is then passed on to the *navigator*."
6. "The *navigator* secretly looks at the two cards in the *captain's logbook* and chooses a card, which they discard into the deep sea **face down**."
7. "The *navigator* leaves their preferred card in the *captain's logbook* and returns the logbook to the captain."
8. "The *captain* reveals the remaining card to his crew and takes the resulting actions."

**Silence rule (p.10):** "During navigation, the selected players **must remain silent and may not give any signs** or signals. Shuffling your navigation cards and discarding a random card to "prove" that you had no choice is **not** allowed."
"**Only after** the captain has revealed the last navigation card, the players can talk about the cards they allegedly drew and discarded."

### Phase 3.1 — Executing the Navigation Card (p.11)

"The *captain* places the chosen card in front of them. This card (and any future cards) represents the captain's official **résumé**. The card remains openly visible in front of them until the end of the game. It will **never** get shuffled into the navigation pile."

"The captain executes the effects of the navigation card in the following order:"
- **I.** "Move the ship in the direction of the colored arrow on the current map space, which corresponds to the color of the navigation card."
- **II.** "If there is an icon on the space the ship moved to, take the map action next. For explanations of the icons read chapter *"Map Actions"*."
- **III.** "At last take the action corresponding to the icon on the navigation card. See the inside of the *captain's logbook* or the chapter *"Navigation Card Actions"* for explanations."

Note the ordering: the map action (II) happens **before** the navigation card action (III), and both happen after the move (I). Also, map actions are carried out "immediately after the captain has moved the ship, but before they perform the action of the navigation card" (p.13).

### Phase 4 — Off-duty (p.11)

"After navigation, certain players have to take a break and receive an off-duty sign. The following table shows who gets an off-duty sign after navigation depending on the number of players at the start of the game."

| Players | Off-duty- Signs | Captain | Lieutenant | Navigator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5-6 | 1 | | | ✓ |
| 7-8 | 2 | | ✓ | ✓ |
| 9-11 | 3 | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

**Steps (p.11):**
- "Give the off-duty signs to the appropriate players. These are the corresponding players who were involved in the **current** navigation."
- "The players who were previously off-duty are now available again for the next navigation."

**Off-duty sign rules (p.11 side box):**
- "As long as an *off-duty* sign is in front of a player, they cannot be chosen as *navigator* or *lieutenant*."
- "*Off-duty* signs are **only** passed on after a successful navigation, **not** after a mutiny."
- "*Off-duty* players can participate in a mutiny and can become captain."
- "If the *captain* has an off-duty sign, the sign won't be relevant as long as that player remains the captain."
- "*Off-duty* signs are ignored if there aren't enough available players left for the *captain* to choose their navigation team."
- "Players "leaving" the ship during the game doesn't change the amount of off-duty signs used."
- Also: "**You can become captain while you are off-duty. If the new captain is off-duty, then the off-duty sign has no effect.**" (p.10)

"The *navigator* and *lieutenant* badges are handed over to the current *captain*." (p.11) — i.e. badges return to the captain at end of phase 3.

### Phase 4.1 / Next round (p.11)

"The current *captain* starts the next round by *"Appointing the navigation team"*."
"In the next and all further rounds, all phases are carried out again: 1. Appointing the navigation team / 2. A question of loyalty / 2.1. Resolving a mutiny / 3. The Navigation / 3.1. Executing the Navigation Card / 4. Off-duty / 4.1. The next game round begins"

### Emergency Navigation (p.12)

"After a *denial of command*, the following steps are performed:"
- "The *captain* must immediately designate an **emergency navigator**."
- "Due to this exceptional situation, the *captain* can even choose an *off-duty* player."
- "The crew can **not** mutiny against the chosen *emergency navigator* (there is no *"A Question of Loyalty"*)."
- "The *lieutenant* stays the same for the emergency navigation."
- "The *captain*, the *lieutenant* and the *emergency navigator* immediately carry out another navigation."
- "If the *emergency navigator* jumps ship as well, the captain keeps choosing emergency navigators until a navigation is successfully completed."

"After a successful emergency navigation, the game continues as normal, starting with the phase *"Appointing the Navigation Team"*."
"If the number of remaining players on the ship ever falls below three, any open position is resolved randomly by the captain. For example, if there is no *lieutenant* then the *captain* draws two cards facedown, shuffles them and randomly puts one into the *captain's logbook* while discarding the other to replace the *lieutenant's* decision."

### Shuffle rule (p.11)

"If there are **fewer than 4 cards** on the draw pile **before** navigation:"
- "Combine the remaining draw pile with the discard pile. Keep all cards facedown in the process and shuffle them thoroughly."
- "The shuffled cards become the new draw pile."

Additional shuffle notes (p.12):
- "The remaining cards **may not** be inspected when the discard pile and draw pile are combined and shuffled."
- "The face up cards in front of the players (**captain's résumé**) are **never** shuffled back into the draw pile."
- "The character cards *Archivist*, *Smuggler* and *Look-Out* can cause you to run out of cards during a navigation process - despite having at least 4 cards before. In that rare case you just reshuffle once the *draw pile* is empty."

### Card effects (p.14, "NAVIGATION CARD ACTIONS")

"The following symbols can be found on the navigation cards. The corresponding action is carried out during the navigation phase (immediately **after** the map action):"

- **Drunk:** "The current *captain* loses their position. The captain role moves clockwise to the next player who has the fewest navigation cards (résumés) in front of them. Pass the *captain's logbook* to the new captain."
- **Mermaid:** "The *captain* chooses another player. That player shuffles the last three discarded navigation cards and secretly looks at them. They may (and should) talk about their findings, but they might be lying."
- **Telescope:** "The *captain* chooses another player. That player looks at the top navigation card of the draw pile. They may put the card back on the draw pile or discard it into the *Deep Sea*."
- **Armed:** "The *navigator* receives 1 gun from the general supply. (These cards are only used on the *long journey* map.)"
- **Disarmed:** "The *navigator* must discard 1 gun from their personal supply (if they have any)."
- **Cult Uprising:** "A cult ritual is carried out at the end of the navigation. To do this, the *captain* must reveal a **cult ritual card**."

### How captain/navigator draw and play (p.10) — summarised as an algorithm

```
captain: draw top 2 from drawPile (face down); keep 1 → logbook, 1 → discard
lieutenant: draw next 2 from drawPile (face down); keep 1 → logbook, 1 → discard
shuffle logbook (close & shake)
navigator: look at both logbook cards; choose 1 → discard; leave the other in logbook
return logbook to captain
captain: reveal remaining card to crew; then execute phases I → II → III
```

**Selection is blind-by-shuffle, not by look:** the captain and lieutenant each choose which of *their own two* cards to contribute, but the navigator sees both contributed cards and throws one away. The captain ends up revealing whichever card the navigator left. The captain/lieutenant may therefore lose information about which of their two cards survives.

### The résumé mechanic

- **Who gets a résumé:** the **captain**, when a navigation resolves. "The *captain* places the chosen card in front of them. This card (and any future cards) represents the captain's official **résumé**." (p.11)
- **When:** on each successful navigation, at phase 3.1 (before the map action and card action resolve; the card is placed first, then effects execute).
- **What it does:**
  1. It is the captain's **public track record** — it accumulates face up in front of the player.
  2. It determines **who becomes captain after a Drunk card**: "the captain role moves clockwise to the next player who has the **fewest navigation cards (résumés)** in front of them."
  3. It is permanent and public: "The card remains openly visible in front of them until the end of the game. It will **never** get shuffled into the navigation pile."
  4. Résumés are the information source players discuss after a navigation — "Discussion about the drawn cards are an important part of the game" (p.10) — but they are the *revealed* card only, not the discarded ones.
- Résumés are **not** transferred or destroyed when a player goes overboard or becomes off-duty (no such rule is given); they stay in front of that player. Résumés never return to the deck ("**never** shuffled back into the draw pile", p.12).

### The Discard pile ("Deep Sea")

- Named the **Deep Sea** / **discard pile** / **map archive**.
- Deluxe edition components (p.3): a **Map Archive Draw Pile** box and a **Deep Sea Discard Pile** box (with the Navigation Cards Storage Box). Basic edition substitutes **1 Discard Pile Card**.
- Cards are discarded **face down** into the Deep Sea: one from the captain, one from the lieutenant, one from the navigator each navigation.
- The discard pile is combined with the draw pile only when the draw pile falls below 4 cards before navigation; the combination is shuffled without inspecting ("The remaining cards **may not** be inspected").
- Discarded cards are the resource for the **Mermaid** action ("shuffles the last three discarded navigation cards") and are not otherwise reachable.

## 6. Mutiny

### Trigger (p.9)
Any player may mutiny during Phase 2. The captain has just appointed lieutenant + navigator. **Every crew member except the captain** secretly places 0+ guns in one fist; then all reveal simultaneously on the captain's count to 3. The captain totals the revealed guns.

### Success thresholds (p.9)

| Players | Required sum of guns for a successful mutiny |
|---|---|
| 5-7 | 3 or more guns |
| 8-9 | 4 or more guns |
| 10-11 | 5 or more guns |

If the total is below the threshold: "all players return their guns back to their personal supply and the captain carries out the next phase of the game: *"The Navigation"*." (p.10) — i.e. a failed mutiny has no cost; appointed team stands.

### Resolving a successful mutiny (p.10)

"If a mutiny is successful, a new captain must be determined. Whoever revealed the **most guns** wins the mutiny and becomes the new captain."

**Tie resolution (verbatim):** "In the case of a tie, all players tied for revealing the most guns raise their hands and the following steps are carried out:"
- "The *captain* still in office selects one of the tied winners who has to lower their hand. The selected player won't become *captain* in this mutiny."
- "If there still are two or more winners in the tie, then the selected player selects another *winner*, who in turn lowers their hand. That player won't become *captain* either."
- "This process is repeated until there is only one player left in the tie."
- "The last remaining *winner* of the mutiny becomes the new *captain*."

So the tie-break is a **sequential elimination by peer choice**, not a re-roll or re-vote: the outgoing captain picks first, then the picked player picks the next, and so on, until one hand remains raised.

### Consequences for the losing side (p.10)

"All players involved in a **successful mutiny** must **discard all their revealed guns** (including the player who has just become *captain*)."

So: every player who revealed ≥1 gun in a successful mutiny loses those revealed guns permanently (returned to the general supply). Players who revealed 0 guns lose nothing. The new captain also loses their revealed guns.

"**All players involved in a successful mutiny**" is broad enough to include any player who revealed guns, whether or not they were tied for the lead — **but note the ambiguity**: the phrase could be read as only the tied winners. The clearer reading, and the one consistent with "discard all their **revealed** guns", is that anyone who showed a gun loses it. Flagged as a minor ambiguity for the engine (default interpretation: all who revealed ≥1 gun lose those guns).

### What happens to the captain after (p.10)

- "The current round ends herewith and the new *captain* starts a new round beginning with the phase *"Appointing the navigation team"*."
- "The new *captain* can also be mutinied against after he announced his navigation team."
- "You can become captain while you are off-duty. If the new captain is off-duty, then the off-duty sign has no effect (for further rules, see chapter "Off-Duty")."
- The old captain simply loses the position and the logbook; there is no other penalty.
- Note: **"*Off-duty* signs are only passed on after a successful navigation, not after a mutiny."** (p.11) — a mutiny round does not move off-duty signs.
- The new captain receives the captain's logbook (Drunk card rule explicitly says "Pass the *captain's logbook* to the new captain."; the mutiny section implies the same by "becomes the new *captain*").

### Guns and public/secret information

- "The number of guns each player has is **secret information during the mutiny**. Outside of the mutiny, however, this is a **public information** and all players must keep their guns visibly on the table at all times." (p.9)
- Off-duty players "can participate in a mutiny and can become captain" (p.11).
- The captain does **not** reveal a gun (categories: "Each crew member (**except the captain**)"). So the captain is immune to losing guns in a mutiny and cannot vote.
- The "Off with the Tongue" player's guns "count normally for the total mutiny score, but while determining the next captain their gun count is always treated as 0 guns" (p.13) — i.e. they can contribute to triggering a mutiny but can never win the captainship via one.

## 7. Denial of Command / Jump Overboard

Verbatim procedure (p.12):

"If the *navigator* doesn't want to execute the commands of the *captain* and the *lieutenant*, he can refuse to do so by jumping overboard. Denial of command is allowed throughout the entire game and takes place during *"The Navigation"* after the navigator has seen both navigation cards."

"**Denial of command** is carried out as follows:"
- "The *navigator* states: *"I will not carry out these commands but jump overboard instead!"*."
- "They place **both navigation cards face down** in the discard pile, instead of passing one back to the captain."
- "The *navigator* is **immediately eliminated** from the game and **must remain silent until the end of the game**. The navigator may **not** reveal their faction nor any knowledge about the discarded navigation cards to the other players."
- "Then an *"Emergency Navigation"* must be carried out."

Additional rules:
- "The Cult Leader can **not** win by jumping overboard as the *navigator*, even if this happens on a *Feed the Kraken* space." (p.12)
- Emergency Navigation procedure as given in §4 above. If the emergency navigator also jumps, repeat until a navigation succeeds.
- If fewer than three players remain on the ship, open positions are resolved randomly by the captain (captain draws two cards facedown, shuffles, one into the logbook, one discarded — replacing the lieutenant's decision).
- Note the denied navigation produces **no résumé** (both cards go to the discard pile) and **no map movement** — the ship does not move, no map action is taken, no card action is taken.
- Elimination does not forfeit the team win: "Players always win together with their team - even if they went overboard before the end of the game." (p.8)

## 8. Cult Rituals

### Trigger (p.15, p.14)

"Every time a navigation card with *"Cult Uprising"* has been played in the navigation, the *captain* must reveal a random facedown *cult ritual* card at the end of the navigation."

The Cult Uprising card itself says: "A cult ritual is carried out at the end of the navigation. To do this, the *captain* must reveal a **cult ritual card**." (p.14)

### Deck contents — 5 cards (p.2, p.15)

"5 Cult Ritual Cards — 3 Conversion to Cult, 1 The cult's guns stash, 1 Cult cabin search"

Setup (p.4, p.6 step 3): "Shuffle the 5 cult ritual cards and place them face down and spread out on the edge of the game board."

### Reveal rule

"Depending on the symbol, one of the following actions is carried out:" — the captain reveals **a random facedown** card (drawn from the spread-out facedown row at the board's edge). Nothing in the rulebook says ritual cards are discarded after use or returned; the cards are simply "spread out on the edge of the game board" and revealed. Flagged: the book does not specify whether revealed ritual cards go back into the pool, stay face up, or are removed — implement as "reveal from the facedown pool"; a revealed card is presumably no longer available (returned face up), but this is **not stated**.

### Card 1 — The Cult's Guns Stash (p.15, p.14)

"The Cult Leader secretly distributes three guns from the supply to any number of players. To do this, place three guns from the supply in the center of the table. Then all players close their eyes and extend their flat hand over the center of the table. If some players cannot reach the center of the table, all players should stand up to make this easier. The Cult Leader opens their eyes and puts the guns in the hands of up to three players. They can choose any player (including themselves), and they can choose to give more than one gun to a player."

### Card 2 — Cult Cabin Search (p.15, p.14)

"The Cult Leader secretly learns about the factions of the current navigation team. To do this, all players close their eyes and the Cult Leader opens their eyes. Keeping their eyes closed, the current captain, *lieutenant* and *navigator* all reveal their faction chip. Make sure to give the Cult Leader 30 seconds to see the three players' factions. The navigation team hides their faction chips again and the Cult Leader closes their eyes. Then everybody opens their eyes again."

(Note the 30-second window is a book instruction.)

### Card 3 — Conversion to Cult (p.15, p.14)

"The Cult Leader converts another player to their Cult. From now on, the chosen player is not a Sailor or Pirate anymore, but a **Cultist** instead. They win or lose together with the Cult Leader."
"See Chapter: *"Becoming a Cultist"*"

### Conversion to Cult — full procedure (p.15, "BECOMING A CULTIST")

"When the cult ritual card *"Conversion to Cult"* is revealed, the *Cult Leader* will secretly add another player to their team by turning them into a Cultist."

**Convertibility:**
- "The Cult Leader can only convert **convertible** players."
- "Every player **who has previously been examined** in a Cabin Search **or Flogging counts as unconvertible**."

(This includes the player examined by *any* Cabin Search — regular map action, or the Cult Cabin Search ritual — and by Flogging. The p.13/p.14 *Important* boxes restate it: "A player affected by the Cabin Search can **not** be turned into a Cultist anymore"; "A player affected by the Flogging can **not** be turned into a Cultist anymore.")

**The captain notes the following text and then recites it to the players:**
1. "*"Every convertible player puts a fist over the center of the table, so that each player could theoretically touch any other fist."*"
2. "*"All players close their eyes."* (including the captain)"
3. "*"Cult leader, open your eyes and choose a convertible player."*"
4. "After approximately 15 seconds: *"Touch your chosen player's fist with your tentacle fingers."*"
5. "*"Selected player, you are now a **Cultist** instead of a Sailor or Pirate. Open your eyes and recognize your new master."*"
6. "After approximately 15 seconds: *"Cultist and Cult Leader, close your eyes now."*"
7. "*"All players open their eyes."*"

"The game continues normally."

**Cultist rules:**
- "The new Cultist loses their previous team membership and is no longer a Pirate or Sailor. They can only win together with the Cult Leader by fulfilling one of the cult victory conditions."
- "Any additional Cultists do **not** open their eyes during this process, only the Cult Leader and the **new** Cultist."

**Cultist count scaling:** only 11-player games start with a Cultist (1 Cultist chip). At 5–10 players there are **no** Cultists at start; Cultists are only ever created by Conversion to Cult. Multiple conversions are possible (3 copies of the card exist). The rulebook does **not** state a maximum number of Cultists — any number of conversions can occur. The 11-player starting Cultist and the Cult Leader "do **not** know each other's identity at the start of the game. They have to find each other during the game, just like the Sailors." (p.8, "There is **no secret gathering of the cult**.")

## 9. Map Actions / Special Actions

General rule (p.13): "Every time the ship reaches a new space, check whether it shows one of the following icons. The corresponding action is then carried out during navigation, immediately after the captain has moved the ship, but before they perform the action of the navigation card. The captain may discuss their options with the crew, but the captain will always make the final decision."

### A. Cabin Search (magnifying glass) — 3× Quick / 4× Long

"The *captain* chooses another player and hands them the cabin search token. The selected player must pass their seag bag to the *captain* for a secret inspection while everybody else closes their eyes. The captain may talk about their findings, but remember that they might also be lying."
**Important:** "A player affected by the Cabin Search can **not** be turned into a Cultist anymore. A player who has already become a Cultist before the cabin search signals this to the captain with a tentacle gesture."
(So: the captain secretly sees the player's true team chip; the captain may lie about it; the examined player becomes permanently unconvertible; and unlike Flogging, no card is placed in front of them.)

### B. Flogging (rope/whip) — 0× Quick / 2× Long

"The Flogging creates a public information for all players. It indicates one of the factions the selected player does **not** belong to."

**Steps:**
- "The *captain* chooses another player to undergo the Flogging and hands them the three small *flogging* cards."
- "The player shuffles the cards facedown, then they look at the cards and choose the card with the colored icon that corresponds to their current faction and places it face down: **Red as a Pirate, blue as a Sailor, yellow/green as a Cult Leader or Cultist**."
- "The flogged player shuffles the other two cards and places them face down on the table."
- "The *captain* randomly picks one of the two cards and reveals it."
- "The card indicates a faction to which the selected player does **not** belong. Place it on top of the *"I am not a..."* card in front of the flogged player."
- "This information is public and the card remains in front of the player until the end of the game."
- "The two cards that remained face down are shuffled face down and set aside."

**Important:** "A player affected by the Flogging can **not** be turned into a Cultist anymore."

**Flogging cards:** "4 Flogging Cards — *"I am not a..."* Card, Pirate Card, Sailor Card, Cult Card" (p.2). Setup p.6 step 7: "Place the *"I am not a..."* card and the 3 small flogging cards next to the map." (Long Journey only.)

Note the clean design: the flogged player reveals a card for their **own actual faction** (removing it from the pool), the captain then picks one of the **two remaining** — which is guaranteed to be a faction the player is *not*. Hence "I am not a X" is truthful.

### C. Off with the Tongue (knife) — 0× Quick / 1× Long

"The *captain* chooses another player and hands them the *off with the tongue* token. That player is no longer allowed to articulate words, but can make sounds and gesticulate. They can also no longer become *captain*."

**Rules (p.13):**
- "When a new *captain* is determined via the *Drunk* action on a navigation card, the player without tongue is ignored."
- "The player continues to participate in mutinies. Their guns count normally for the total mutiny score, but while determining the next *captain* their gun count is always treated as 0 guns."

### D. Feed the Kraken (octopus) — 2× Quick / 3× Long

"When they have made up their mind, the *captain* chooses another player and places the *Feed the Kraken* token in front of them. That player is **immediately eliminated** from the game and **must not speak anymore** or reveal their faction."

**Important:** "The *captain* must **not** choose himself."
"If the Cult Leader is selected to be fed to the Kraken, the **Cult Leader and all of their Cultists immediately win the game**."

### E. Summary of captain-facing special actions

| Icon | Name | Quick | Long | Target chosen by | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🔍 | Cabin Search | 3 | 4 | captain picks another player | – | captain secretly views target's faction chip; target becomes unconvertible |
| 🪢 | Flogging | 0 | 2 | captain picks another player | – | public "I am not a X" card placed in front of target; target becomes unconvertible |
| 🔪 | Off with the Tongue | 0 | 1 | captain picks another player | – | target silenced (sounds only, no words), can never be captain |
| 🐙 | Feed the Kraken | 2 | 3 | captain picks another player, **not self** | – | target eliminated; if target is Cult Leader → Cult wins immediately |

**Ambiguity flagged:** the rulebook does not state whether a map action token is consumed/returned once used. Since tokens are placed on specific spaces and the ship can revisit, the natural reading is that the icon stays on the space and the action can be taken again each time the ship enters that space. The book does not say this explicitly.

## 10. Other Mechanics

### Elimination / Silence

- **Navigator who denies command:** "immediately eliminated from the game and must remain silent until the end of the game", may not reveal faction or knowledge of the discarded cards (p.12).
- **Feed the Kraken target:** "immediately eliminated from the game and must not speak anymore or reveal their faction" (p.14).
- Both remain eligible for their team's victory: "Players always win together with their team - even if they went overboard before the end of the game." (p.8)
- Eliminated players' guns: the rulebook gives **no** rule for reallocating an eliminated player's guns to the supply or to anyone else. Their chips/résumés also stay untransferred. "Players "leaving" the ship during the game doesn't change the amount of off-duty signs used." (p.11)
- **Off with the Tongue** is not elimination — the player still plays, votes in mutinies, and can hold office, but cannot win a captainship and cannot speak words.

### The Secret Gathering of the Pirates (before play, p.8)

"Before the game begins, the Pirates will secretly and silently find out who their Pirate teammates are. For this, one player notes the following text and delivers it to the players with eyes closed:"
- "*"All players close their eyes."*"
- "*"Pirates, open your eyes and memorize your team members."*"
- "*The Pirates have about 20 seconds to find each other ...*"
- "*"Pirates, close your eyes."*"
- "*"All players now open their eyes."* - The journey begins!"

"There is **no secret gathering of the cult**. In a game with 11 players, the Cult Leader and the first Cultist do **not** know each other's identity at the start of the game. They have to find each other during the game, just like the Sailors."

### Captain / character card dealing (p.8)

"**After that the players receive their character cards:**"
- "Set aside the *"Captain"* card, then shuffle all the other character cards and set aside one card less than your number of players. Add the *"Captain"* card to them and shuffle those cards facedown."
- "Deal one card to each player face down. Players look at their own card without revealing it."
- "The player with the *"Captain"* card reveals it immediately. They are the first captain in this game and receive a random new character card."
- "The *captain* places the *captain's logbook* in front of them, as well as the *lieutenant* badge and *navigator* badge."

**Worked example (mine, not the book's):** for 7 players, set aside 7−1 = 6 non-Captain cards, add the Captain card → 7 cards shuffled, one each. The Captain-card holder announces, swaps their Captain card for a random unused card, and is the first captain.

**Character cards (22 cards, p.2):** "Each player receives a character card at the start of the game. These remain facedown in front of the players, until they are used. Players can reveal their character card strategically at any point in the game to receive a unique effect (timing restrictions may apply)."

"**HOW TO ACTIVATE YOUR CHARACTER CARD:** To activate your character card, flip and reveal the card. Only unflipped (facedown) character cards may be activated. After activation, your character card remains revealed in front of you. A character can only be activated once, unless it gets turned facedown by an effect."

Box note (p.16): "Most character cards can only be used **once**, but **at any time**. Some have timing restrictions, and very few have recurring or ongoing effects. All exceptions are explained on the respective character cards. Some characters work better with certain player numbers. For example, we suggest to avoid using Debt Collector, Minstrel and Mentor in games with 5-6 players."

**The individual character card texts are NOT printed in this rulebook** — they are printed on the cards themselves. Only these three clarifications are given (p.16, "NOTES ON SOME CHARACTER CARDS"):
- **Agitator:** "Choosing the captain or players chosen by the *"Minstrel"* has no effect because they do not participate in a mutiny."
- **Chief Cook:** "The new *captain* appoints a new navigation team."
- **Instigator:** "You can not choose the captain, but you can choose a player chosen by the *"Minstrel"*. Turning the *"Instigator"* facedown means that it can be activated again later in the game."

Character cards named anywhere in the book: **Captain, Agitator, Chief Cook, Instigator, Minstrel, Debt Collector, Mentor, Archivist, Smuggler, Look-Out**. The remaining character names are not listed in the rulebook. **FLAGGED as a gap** — the engine will need the 22 character cards photographed separately, or the 4 Crew Overview Cards.

### Round counter / "nth round" effects

There is **no** round counter and there are **no** "nth round" effects in the rulebook. The only progression clock is the ship's position on the board. Cards that reshuffle are triggered by draw-pile depth (< 4 cards), not by round number.

### End-of-game scoring

There is **none**. "The navigation rounds are repeated until one of the victory conditions is met" (p.13). At the end, players reveal their faction chips from their sea bags (p.13). Victory is whole-team binary. No points, no tie-breakers, no partial credit.

### Off-duty interaction summary (all in one place)

- Off-duty cannot be appointed lieutenant or navigator (p.9).
- Off-duty signs move only after a successful navigation, not after a mutiny (p.11).
- Off-duty players may mutiny and may become captain (p.11).
- If the captain is off-duty, the sign is inert while they remain captain (p.11).
- Off-duty signs are ignored if there are not enough available players to staff the team (p.11).
- Eliminated players do not reduce the off-duty sign count (p.11).
- The **emergency navigator may be off-duty** (p.12).

## 12. Explicit Ambiguities / Gaps (do not guess these)

1. **Exact map space count and per-space arrow directions are NOT in the rulebook.** Must be transcribed from the physical board. (This matches the project's existing "Map topology blocker" memory note.)
2. **Board art vs. victory text contradiction:** the west hex is labelled "SAILOR VICTORY" and the east hex "PIRATE VICTORY" on pp.4–7, while the text (p.8) says Sailors win "Bluewater Bay, the blue area in the east" and Pirates win "Crimson Cove, the red area in the west". Verify against the physical board before hard-coding win regions.
3. **Who exactly loses guns in a successful mutiny** — "all players involved in a successful mutiny" is not explicit about whether that means only the tied winners or everyone who revealed. Default: everyone who revealed ≥1 gun.
4. **Cult ritual cards after reveal** — the book never says whether a revealed ritual card is removed, turned face up and set aside, or returned to the facedown pool.
5. **Map action tokens after use** — never says whether an icon is consumed. Default: re-usable on re-entry to the space.
6. **Character cards' individual texts are not in the rulebook** (22 cards, only 3 notes given). Need card photos.
7. **The `7th` / `8th` "pages"** — image 17 is a full-page "Strategy Advice" essay (no printed page number) that contains no rules, only tactics. Image 18 is a two-column condensation of pp.13–14 (Map Actions + Cult Ritual Cards), also without a printed number; its content matches pp.13–14 exactly with one addition: p.18's Cult Cabin Search box says "Make sure to give the Cult Leader **30 seconds**", where p.15 says "**30 seconds**" as well — both agree. No unique rules appear on image 18.
8. **5-player bag preparation** is slightly odd (6 bags prepared, 1 removed unseen); the number of Sailor/Pirate chips prepared for other counts is not restated, but is implied to equal the table's counts.
9. **Nothing defines what happens to a gun-supply shortage** (40 guns total, 11 players × 3 = 33 starting, + Armed cards + Cult's Guns Stash + supply-line refills). Recommend tracking the general supply as a finite pool of 40 in the engine as printed, but the book sets no explicit cap behavior for exhaustion.
